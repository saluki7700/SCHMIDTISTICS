The three dashed lines

Lydia Jane Voudy to William Voudy and Dorcas Booker. No document yet found says she was their daughter. Everything above her — Voudy, Varrell, Sadler and the Isles of Shoals — rests on this one link.

Dorcas Booker to Aaron Booker. Also unstated. The strongest evidence is a deed of 1868 naming Aaron’s former farm and Dorcas’s life estate in one sentence — and it still stops short of saying she was his daughter. The Booker, Moore and Adams lines hang from it.

John H. Dudley’s father. William Dudley married Caty Amee at Kittery in 1803 and is the only Dudley household in the town in 1820 and 1830. Circumstantial, not a record.

Why the Schmidt line stops after two rows

Not for want of looking. Otto Schmidt described himself in 1842 as “not a native of this country, but a Prussian”, and after a letter of 9 June that year he leaves the record entirely. His own father is named once — Charles Louis Schmidt, barrister — and no Inn of Court in London has heard of him. That search belongs on the continent.

A note on the dates

Years only, and “unknown” means unknown rather than guessed. Three differ from the working pedigree because a document says otherwise: Otto Schmidt born about 1801 (two hands record him as forty-one in 1842); John H. Dudley born 1806 (the schooner Pacific’s own watch list, not his gravestone, which says 1804 and was cut at least thirteen years after he died); and Aaron Booker born 1758 (the York town register, not the ages he swore to, which run four to seven years old).
